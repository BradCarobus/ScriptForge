Step 01 — Foundation

What's your common goal
with the viewer?

Before a single word is written, you need a shared destination. This isn't about your video topic — it's about what you and your viewer are both striving toward together.

A rough title — just to ground us. You'll refine it in Packaging.

Not what you want to teach — what they're walking away with. Be specific.

Channel growth, trust, sales, authority? Be honest.

This overlap IS the common goal. The foundation of your entire video.

Step 02 — The Problem

Surface issue.
Deeper wound.

Great scripts address two problems simultaneously. The surface problem is what they Googled. The deeper problem is the emotional struggle underneath — the one they'd never admit out loud.

Example: Surface = "My Notion setup keeps breaking." Deeper = "I'm terrified I'm just lazy and no system will ever work for me."

The concrete, Googleable thing they're struggling with.

The identity-level fear or frustration underneath. What does failing at this make them feel about themselves?

A scene, scenario, or moment that makes them feel seen — without spelling it out.

Step 03 — Packaging

What promise
are you making?

Your thumbnail and title make a promise. Your script must deliver exactly that promise — nothing more, nothing less. Mismatches destroy trust and tank retention.

What visual image makes the same promise? Describe it.

Finish this: "By the end, the viewer will have..." — be precise, not vague.

Anti-promise check. Knowing what breaks trust helps you avoid it.

Step 04 — Audience Avatars

Who specifically
is watching?

Define 2–3 specific personas. Real people, not demographics. These avatars decide: how complex your explanations are, what examples land, and what tone feels natural.

Avatar 1 — Primary Viewer
Avatar 2 — Secondary Viewer

Given your avatars, how much do you explain vs. assume they know?

Step 05 — Research

What gaps did
others leave?

Study the top videos on your topic. What did they miss? What questions did commenters ask that weren't answered? Your unique insight fills those gaps.

List the 3–5 videos that already cover this. What do they do well?

These are your gold. Real questions = real gaps.

What do YOU know that others don't — from personal experience, credentials, or perspective?

Step 06 — Structure & Arc

Map the
transformation.

Every great video has a clear before and after. Then layer the Hero's Journey: conflict, stakes, trials, and transformation. This isn't just for storytelling — it's what keeps viewers watching.

Emotionally and practically — where are they when they press play?

What has shifted — emotionally and practically?

Step 07 — Universe Building

Where does this fit
in your larger story?

The best channels aren't collections of videos — they're serialized universes. How does this video connect to your past and future content? Like the MCU, each video should feel like an episode in something larger.

A line in the script that plants the seed for the next episode without feeling forced.

Step 08 — Draft Notes

Before you
write a word.

Use AI to roast your outline (not write your script). Then write fast and messy. Notes on rhythm, show-don't-tell moments, and pacing go here — the stuff to keep in mind during drafting.

Write the prompt you'll use to have Claude critique your outline before you draft. What should it look for?

List 2–4 moments where instead of stating something, you'll let the viewer feel/discover it.

Where do you want to speed up? Slow down? Use very short sentences? Vary the pace intentionally.
