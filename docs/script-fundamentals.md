# Script Fundamentals — The ScriptForge Framework

> Saved from the original ScriptForge outline builder (the 8-step wizard that used to be the whole site).
> Keep this as the reference for a future "script design" mode. Every step below was a page in that wizard,
> and every bullet was a field you filled in.

**Golden rule:** don't start scripting until the outline is done. Then write fast, write messy, don't stop.

---

## 1. Foundation — the common goal

Before a single word is written, you need a shared destination. Not your topic — what you and the viewer
are *both* striving toward.

- **Working title** — a rough title just to ground you. You'll refine it in Packaging.
- **What does the viewer want to achieve after watching?** Not what you want to teach — what they walk away with. Be specific.
- **What do YOU want to achieve with this video?** Channel growth, trust, sales, authority? Be honest.
- **Where do these two goals overlap?** That overlap *is* the common goal — the foundation of the whole video.

## 2. The Problem — surface issue, deeper wound

Great scripts address two problems at once. The surface problem is what they Googled. The deeper problem is
the emotional struggle underneath — the one they'd never admit out loud.

> Example: Surface = "My Notion setup keeps breaking." Deeper = "I'm terrified I'm just lazy and no system will ever work for me."

- **Surface problem (technical)** — the concrete, Googleable thing they're struggling with.
- **Deeper problem (emotional)** — the identity-level fear underneath. What does failing at this make them feel about themselves?
- **How will you hint at the deeper problem without stating it? (show, don't tell)** — a scene, scenario, or moment that makes them feel seen without spelling it out.

## 3. Packaging — the promise

The title and thumbnail make a promise. The script must deliver exactly that promise — nothing more, nothing
less. Mismatches destroy trust and tank retention.

- **Refined title (the promise)**
- **Thumbnail concept** — what visual image makes the *same* promise?
- **Exact promise the video must fulfill** — finish this: "By the end, the viewer will have…" Precise, not vague.
- **Anti-promise check** — what would make someone feel the video didn't deliver? Knowing what breaks trust helps you avoid it.

## 4. Audience Avatars — who specifically is watching

Define 2–3 specific personas. Real people, not demographics. Avatars decide how complex your explanations
are, what examples land, and what tone feels natural.

For each avatar (Primary, Secondary…):
- Name / archetype
- Background
- What they already know
- Their frustration, in their own words

Then make the **content complexity decision**: given these avatars, how much do you explain vs. assume they know?

## 5. Research — the gaps others left

Study the top videos on the topic. What did they miss? What did commenters ask that never got answered?
Your unique insight fills those gaps.

- **Top competing videos** — the 3–5 that already cover this. What do they do well?
- **Most common unanswered comments/questions** — these are gold. Real questions = real gaps.
- **Your unique angle / unfair advantage** — what do you know from experience, credentials, or perspective that others don't?

## 6. Structure & Arc — map the transformation

Every great video has a clear before and after. Layer the Hero's Journey on top — conflict, stakes, trials,
transformation. It's not just storytelling; it's what keeps people watching.

- **Viewer's state at the START** — emotionally and practically, where are they when they press play?
- **Viewer's state at the END** — what has shifted?

Hero's Journey map:

| Beat | Role | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| ⚡ The Ordinary World / **Hook** | grab attention, set the scene | 0:00–0:30 |
| 🔥 The Call / **Stakes** | the conflict — why this matters | right after the hook |
| 🗺️ The Trials / **Main Content** | the body of the video | the middle |
| ✨ The Transformation / **Payoff** | the resolution — deliver the promise | the end |

## 7. Universe Building — the larger story

The best channels aren't collections of videos — they're serialized universes. Like the MCU, each video
should feel like an episode in something bigger.

- What's the overarching theme / mission of the channel?
- What past video does this reference or build on?
- What future video does this set up?
- **The connection line** — a line in the script that plants the seed for the next episode without feeling forced (tease, callback, CTA).

## 8. Draft Notes — before you write a word

Use AI to *roast your outline* (not write your script). Then write fast and messy.

- **AI roast prompt** — the prompt you'll use to have Claude critique your outline. What should it look for?
- **Show-don't-tell moments** — 2–4 places where instead of stating something, you let the viewer feel or discover it.
- **Rhythm notes** — where to speed up, slow down, use very short sentences. Vary pace on purpose.
- **Anything else** to keep in mind while drafting.

---

## Extra principles (used throughout the new ScriptForge hub)

- **But / Therefore, never "and then".** Connect story beats with *but* (a complication) or *therefore*
  (a consequence). Beats joined by "and then" are a list, not a story — that's where viewers click away.
- **Hook = promise + stakes in the first 30 seconds.** Confirm the click (deliver on the thumbnail
  immediately), then open a loop the viewer needs closed.
- **Resolution must pay off the exact packaging promise.**
- **Runtime estimate:** spoken script runs about 150 words per minute.
